Exercise & Fitness

Build strength, endurance, and energy with fitness strategies that fit your lifestyle.

Why Exercise Matters in Your 20s and 30s

Regular physical activity is one of the most impactful things you can do for your health. In your 20s and 30s, exercise helps you build a strong foundation that will benefit you for decades to come.

Benefits of regular exercise include:

  • Physical health: Stronger muscles and bones, better cardiovascular health, weight management, and improved immune function.
  • Mental health: Reduced anxiety and depression, improved mood, better stress management, and enhanced self-esteem.
  • Cognitive benefits: Better focus, improved memory, enhanced creativity, and sharper thinking.
  • Energy levels: Regular exercise actually increases your energy rather than depleting it.
  • Better sleep: Physical activity promotes deeper, more restorative sleep.

Recommended Activity Levels

Health organizations recommend the following for adults aged 18-35:

150+ minutes of moderate aerobic activity per week
2+ days of strength training per week
75 minutes of vigorous activity as alternative
10K steps daily is a good baseline goal

Starting Out?

If you're new to exercise, start with just 10-15 minutes a day and gradually increase. Any movement is better than none, and building the habit matters more than intensity at first.

Types of Exercise to Include

A well-rounded fitness routine includes different types of exercise, each offering unique benefits:

  • Cardiovascular/Aerobic: Running, cycling, swimming, dancing, or brisk walking. Improves heart health and burns calories.
  • Strength Training: Weight lifting, resistance bands, or bodyweight exercises. Builds muscle, boosts metabolism, and strengthens bones.
  • Flexibility: Stretching, yoga, or Pilates. Improves range of motion, reduces injury risk, and relieves muscle tension.
  • Balance: Yoga, tai chi, or single-leg exercises. Important for overall functional fitness and injury prevention.
  • HIIT: High-intensity interval training. Efficient for burning calories and improving cardiovascular fitness in less time.

Try to incorporate a mix throughout your week for comprehensive fitness benefits.

Fitting Exercise Into a Busy Life

The number one barrier to exercise is time. Here are strategies to make fitness work with your schedule:

  • Schedule it: Treat workouts like important meetings. Put them in your calendar and protect that time.
  • Morning workouts: Exercise before other commitments can derail your plans. Many find this sets a positive tone for the day.
  • Active commuting: Walk, bike, or get off public transit a few stops early. Build activity into transportation.
  • Short bursts count: Three 10-minute walks provide similar benefits to one 30-minute session.
  • Lunch breaks: Use part of your lunch break for a quick workout or walk.
  • Home workouts: No gym? No problem. Bodyweight exercises, online videos, and minimal equipment can deliver great results.
  • Social exercise: Combine social time with physical activity. Walk with friends, join recreational sports leagues, or take classes together.

Building a Sustainable Routine

The best exercise routine is one you'll actually stick with. Here's how to build lasting habits:

  • Start small: Ambitious goals often lead to burnout. Begin with manageable commitments and build from there.
  • Find activities you enjoy: Exercise shouldn't feel like punishment. Experiment until you find forms of movement you genuinely like.
  • Track progress: Use apps, journals, or simple notes to monitor your activity. Seeing progress is motivating.
  • Have backup plans: Can't make it to the gym? Have a home workout ready. Raining outside? Know your indoor options.
  • Rest and recover: Rest days are essential. They allow your body to repair and grow stronger. Include at least 1-2 rest days per week.
  • Don't compare: Focus on your own progress, not others. Everyone's fitness journey is different.

The Two-Day Rule

Try not to skip more than two days of exercise in a row. This helps maintain momentum without being overly rigid.

Exercise Safety Tips

Stay safe while staying active:

  • Warm up: Always spend 5-10 minutes warming up before intense exercise to prepare your muscles and reduce injury risk.
  • Proper form: Quality matters more than quantity. Learn correct form, especially for strength training exercises.
  • Progress gradually: Increase intensity, duration, or weight by no more than 10% per week to avoid overuse injuries.
  • Listen to your body: Pain is different from discomfort. Sharp or persistent pain means stop and evaluate.
  • Stay hydrated: Drink water before, during, and after exercise, especially in hot weather.
  • Cool down: End workouts with light movement and stretching to help your body recover.
